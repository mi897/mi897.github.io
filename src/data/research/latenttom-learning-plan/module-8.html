Module 8: Extension, consensus without a shared camera about 10 hours, optional

In the paper, the consensus embedding comes from a fixed third-person camera that both arms see. This extension asks whether the arms can still reach useful consensus when that camera is removed, so the only way to agree is through what each arm sees of the shared scene from its own viewpoint. It is my own research question, not something the paper tests, and it is not counted in the 90 hours.

What to learn

  • What the consensus encoder is actually given. Re-read the paper and the official code to confirm which inputs feed the consensus branch, and what each arm's own sensors can see of the other arm and of the object.
  • Why the third-person view helps: both arms get nearly the same input, so agreement is easy. Without it, each arm sees a different, partial view and agreement has to be learned through the loss, which is where the cohomology and ToM terms are tested.
  • The measuring tools from Module 3: embedding agreement and the linear probe to block pose.
My hypotheses, to be tested rather than assumed: removing the camera lowers success most for the naive baseline; the cohomology and ToM terms recover some of the gap by forcing both arms to encode the same scene state; and the Laplacian update helps most when one arm's view of the block is poor.
