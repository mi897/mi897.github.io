Overview: what you are rebuilding

LatentToM lets two robot arms cooperate without a central controller, and this plan has you rebuild it in a 2D simulation in about 90 hours. The original used two real arms and a real camera rig, so every project here uses a simulated stand-in.

How a decision flows

  1. Each arm sees two inputs: its own wrist camera plus pose (ego), and one shared third-person camera (consensus).
  2. Two encoders turn these into an ego embedding and a consensus embedding.
  3. In training only, a cohomology loss pulls the arms' consensus embeddings together. A theory-of-mind decoder and a confidence-based directional loss keep those embeddings informative.
  4. Each arm's own diffusion policy turns its embeddings into an action chunk.
  5. At run time, if the arms can communicate, they swap consensus embeddings and average them for T rounds. That is the sheaf Laplacian update.
